Problem Statement

Loan default poses a significant financial risk for lending institutions, impacting profitability and capital recovery. 
Understanding the factors contributing to loan defaults is essential for risk assessment and strategic decision-making. 
The dataset contains various borrower and loan-related attributes, and the challenge is to identify key patterns, relationships,
and trends that differentiate defaulted loans from successfully repaid ones.

Objective

The main objective of this study is to perform an in-depth exploratory data analysis (EDA) to:
Understand the structure and quality of the loan dataset.
Identify relationships between borrower/loan attributes and the likelihood of default.
Detect trends, correlations, and anomalies that can inform predictive modeling.
Prepare the dataset through cleaning and preprocessing for future machine learning applications to predict loan defaults.

Conclusion
The analysis revealed distinct differences between defaulted and non-defaulted loans in terms of disbursal dates, 
loan amounts, and certain borrower characteristics such as employment type. Missing data handling and correlation 
analysis provided insights into which features are most relevant for risk prediction. Visual patterns suggest that loan 
disbursal timing and higher loan amounts may correlate with increased default risk. These findings form the foundation 
for building predictive models, enabling financial institutions to improve risk mitigation strategies and enhance loan approval decision-making.
